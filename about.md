# About Me

My journey into cybersecurity started with a broken 1970s stereo receiver that still ran on vacuum tubes. As a teenager, diagnosing and fixing that temperamental piece of equipment sparked something in me—a fascination with understanding how things work, why they break, and how to make them better. Little did I know that curiosity would shape the next three decades of my career.

## A Career Built on Curiosity

I've been fortunate to spend almost 30 years in the IT world since 1998, with over 25 of those years focused entirely on cybersecurity. My path began as a Windows system administrator (yes, I still have my MSCE for Windows NT certificate somewhere), but it was Stephen Northcutt's "Network Intrusion Detection: An Analysts' Handbook" that truly changed everything. The idea of monitoring network traffic and extracting meaningful data from the digital chaos fascinated me in ways I hadn't expected.

Today, I serve as Director of IT Security for Business Area Enterprise Wireless Solutions (BEWS), a division of Ericsson, where I get to tackle the complex challenges of securing enterprise wireless infrastructure. But my career has taken me through virtually every corner of cybersecurity—from intrusion detection systems and vulnerability management to risk assessment, penetration testing, and vendor security management. I've had the privilege of building multi-million dollar cybersecurity programs for Fortune 100 companies, including the top internet service and cable providers in the country.

## Teaching the Next Generation

Beyond my corporate role, I spend evenings and weekends doing something I'm equally passionate about—teaching cybersecurity at Regis University. There's something energizing about working with graduate and undergraduate students who bring fresh perspectives to our field. I currently teach courses in advancing to cybersecurity, computer and network forensics, offensive security, and malware analysis.

I've written or completely rewritten several courses, including computer forensics at both graduate and undergraduate levels, and I'm currently working on modernizing our malware analysis curriculum. One project I'm particularly proud of is designing and deploying a competition scenario for the Rocky Mountain Regional Collegiate Cyber Defense Competition—complete with a detailed narrative backstory and multiple vulnerable servers that challenge students to think like both attackers and defenders.

## Leadership Through Diversity

My approach to leadership was forged in the U.S. Army, but it's been refined through decades of leading teams in high-stakes corporate environments. I've learned that the best security programs aren't just about technology—they're about people. That's why I'm passionate about building diverse, inclusive teams and actively mentor through my ally membership with Women in Security (WyCIS).

Some of the achievements I'm most proud of include expanding security program coverage by 300% in under three years, achieving 14 consecutive clean PCI attestations, and implementing application security and risk assessment programs that have saved millions in operational costs. But honestly, what energizes me most is seeing team members grow and succeed.

## Beyond the Office

When I'm not deep in threat models or grading forensics assignments, you'll likely find me tinkering with single board computers. My obsession with SBCs started with a purpose-built controller for haunted house automation (yes, really) and has since expanded to include Arduino, ESP32s, Raspberry Pis, and whatever new board catches my eye. I've designed and operated automated attractions for multiple non-profit haunted houses, including what was once the largest non-profit haunted attraction in Colorado.

## Continuous Learning

This field never stops evolving, which is exactly what I love about it. I've completed numerous training courses through SANS and Black Hat/DEF CON, earned my CISSP and CISM certifications alongside my MSCIT from Regis, and I'm always exploring new technologies and methodologies. Whether it's understanding the latest attack vectors or figuring out how to secure emerging wireless technologies, there's always something new to learn.

## Looking Forward

After almost 30 years in this business, I'm as excited about cybersecurity's future as I was when I first discovered network intrusion detection. The threats are more sophisticated, the technology more complex, but the fundamental mission remains the same—protecting what matters most while enabling innovation and growth.

Whether you're looking for insights on building resilient security programs, developing the next generation of cybersecurity professionals, or just want to chat about the latest in threat intelligence, I'd love to connect.
